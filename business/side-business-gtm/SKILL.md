# Side-Business GTM

## When to Use

Use when developing, validating, benchmarking, or closing work on a
side-revenue business for the operator (opportunity research, GTM, outreach
waves, pricing, validation), or when the operator names a pursuit from this
class.

Workflow for the recurring class: turning the operator's skills into a
low-overhead side revenue business. AI may serve as back-office tooling only;
the business model itself must never depend on AI output being good.

## Standing constraints (every instance)

- Model must survive the question: "does this still sell if the buyer
  believes AI output is worthless?" If no, kill or reframe.
- No commodity dropshipping, no paid-ads-from-day-one, no committed retainer
  ops unless the operator explicitly reopens that door. Record the operator's
  standing no-list (what is off the table, and why) in your operator profile
  and apply it before ranking anything.
- Time budget and capital envelope come first; every ranked opportunity
  carries its own validation cost estimate and is rejected over it.
- Set the operator's lens once and reuse it: their training, distinctive
  methods, and domain fluency recorded as a fill-in profile
  (`<LENS: the operator's distinctive training and methods, e.g. human
  factors, quantitative methods, research methodology, engineering>`), plus
  their appetite for learning new domains. Do not pigeonhole recommendations
  into the operator's current professional niche. Apply the lens ACROSS
  domains, and say so when a cross-domain match wins.

## Procedure

1. **Option widening.** Generate a candidate list well above the target
   count, then run an adversarial kill-pass with named gates applied in
   order: (a) demand evidence must cite actual comparable purchases or
   prices, not category adjacency; (b) the no-AI gate above; (c)
   maintenance-fit against the hours envelope; (d) lens-advantage gate, a
   generic freelancer without the operator's recorded training must fail to
   replicate it in under 20 h. Each verdict carries one line of cited
   evidence; KILL is a valid outcome and gets documented, not padded. Rank
   survivors with an explicit one-sentence justification per ordering pair.
2. **GTM research per surviving opportunity**, dispatched to a parallel
   subagent with a self-contained brief (success criteria, allowed tools, and
   an output contract stated up front; verify its claims before use).
   Required sections: demand map citing how buyers actually source and pay
   today, ICP, ranked channels with ordering justification, pricing anchored
   to retrieved incumbent rates, regulatory/licensing note,
   portfolio-fit requirements, and a named first-10 outreach list gated on
   publicly visible contacts only. Every claim carries a URL plus a
   confidence tag; unsupported claims live in a quarantine appendix and
   never in pricing or outreach lists.
3. **Human recheck gate (do not skip).** Before any real-world send, read the
   deliverable fully and verify a sample of claims yourself: confirm each
   outreach target's practice area matches the ICP on a page you fetch
   yourself (runs have listed principals under the wrong practice area),
   confirm contact emails render, and check for wrong salutations or
   miscounts. Fix the artifact in place; strike failed targets rather than
   padding with replacements.
4. **Validation campaign**, cheapest first: under-$100 experiments that
   generate real buyer signals (forum answers, small agreement studies, a
   2-page capability brief plus a short email round). Define the success gate
   (for example, 3 replies or 1 paid engagement) before starting. No send
   without the operator's clearance on the regulatory posture section.
5. **Outreach pack artifact**: a file with one adapted email per target
   matched to that firm's actual practice (never a generic template sent
   twice), each with subject, expected objection and reply, and a per-send
   checklist. The operator sends from their own mailbox themselves; never
   send on their behalf without an explicit per-wave instruction.
